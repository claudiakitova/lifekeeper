# Collecting your data, and how much is enough

## How much to collect

You don't need everything. Each level below is useful on its own, so pick the one that fits your time.

| Level | Time | What to collect | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | an afternoon | Your phone's photos, your main chat app, and 10–20 lines of your own notes in `my_events.csv`: moves, jobs, people, births, losses | A timeline of where you were and who you talked to, year by year, with the main events in your words |
| Good | a week | Add Google Takeout (location history, calendar, YouTube), your social media posts, a second chat app, and scans of key documents (IDs, diplomas, medical results) | Places and trips, what you wrote over the years, and the papers you'd need in an emergency |
| Rich | ongoing | Add health apps, bank statements, email, music history, and a one-line daily note | Patterns across years, and a baseline for noticing change |

### If the archive is for memory loss

The smallest set that helps (see [Memory loss](memory-loss.md)):

- 20–50 photos, with names on faces and the place and year
- the 10 most important people, with how the person knows them
- the places they've lived, and their work
- 10 songs they love
- daily routines, likes and dislikes, what comforts them and what upsets them
- the papers that matter: power of attorney, advance directive, medical summary

### If you want a baseline for noticing change

Your own writing over time is the baseline. If you already write messages, posts or notes, keep exporting them. If you don't, write a short weekly note in your own words (about 100 words, in the same language each time).

## How to collect

### Export instead of connecting

Download a copy of your data instead of only giving a service access to it. A copy stays with you, and access can be withdrawn at any time. ([Why](why-not-just-connect-ai.md))

### Ask for the right format

- Choose JSON (machine-readable) over HTML when there's a choice.
- Choose "all time" for the date range and "high quality" for media.
- Include photos, voice messages and videos if they matter to you. They make the export big.

### Start with the slow ones

Google Takeout, Facebook and Instagram take hours to days. Request them first, then do the quick ones while you wait. [Step-by-step guides](download-your-data/).

### Keep originals exactly as they came

- One folder per source, named with the date of the export: `exports/telegram-2026-10/`.
- Don't rename, sort or "clean" anything inside. Corrections go in a separate layer.
- Zips can stay zipped. Lifekeeper reads inside them.

### Photos: keep their dates

Copy photos straight from the phone, or use the cloud's original-quality download. Photos sent through messengers often lose their date and place, so keep the originals.

### Paper: scan what matters first

Scan medical results, legal papers, diplomas and letters first. Make one PDF per document. You don't need to name them.

### Old photo albums: write down the year

Scanned photos carry no date. Put the year in the file name or in a folder's name (`1985 summer.jpg`, or a folder called `1985`) and Lifekeeper shows them under that year. Write down who is in each photo while someone still remembers.

### Old devices: copy them soon

Old phones, laptops and USB drives hold years that were never exported, and they fail without warning. Copy them whole.

### Other people's data

If a parent's photos or a friend's chats go into your archive, ask first, keep them in their own folder, and don't publish them.

## Keep it going

- Export twice a year. Put two reminders in your calendar. Keep every export, because each one is a snapshot and services delete old data.
- Write one line a day if you can, in a note app or a mood app with a note field.
- Write one note a year: what happened, who mattered, what changed.
- Back up after every new export. See [Backup](backup.md).
