# Readable on a hard day

A page in an archive should be easy to read when you're tired, ill or grieving, or when your memory isn't what it was. These are the rules I write pages by, and the checklist I use to review them.

## Words

- Use plain language. Keep system words off the page: "records", "parsed", "source library", "model", "low confidence". Don't explain how the data was made.
- Write in the first person: "I moved to Berlin", "my CV", "Anna and I". Not "the user", and not your own name in the third person.
- Use one language per page and one spelling per name. If your archive mixes languages, give every title and label in the page's language. Translate generic words and transliterate names. A document keeps its own language inside, but its title follows the page.
- Cut extra words. "PhD in physics", not "PhD in physics (study)". "read Apr 26, 2026", not "read · read Apr 26".
- Keep labels and buttons short and lowercase, except names.

## Titles

- A title says what the thing is. A file name isn't a title: not "IMG_2041", "Scan 19" or "Recording 12.06".
- Tell versions apart by when they were saved ("CV (April 2024)") or by what differs, not by a number copied from inside the file.
- No two entries should look the same. Add what tells them apart, or merge them if they're the same thing.
- A summary is one or two sentences, true to the file, and says whose document it is. A parent's medical scan isn't yours.
- A message says who is speaking to whom: "me → Anna", "Anna → me".

## Dates and numbers

- Write dates so they read easily: "Apr 14, 2026", "Dec 2022", "Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 17:08". Not "2026-04-14" or "04/14/2026".
- Show one date, not a range from a day to the same day.
- Use thousands separators: 12,500. Keep units as the original printed them.
- Show money in the one currency you think in, with the original in brackets when it differs.
- Recount every count from its source before showing it.

## Order

- Put things where they belong: cover letters with cover letters, certificates together, odds and ends in "other".
- Name folders the way a person would: no numbering, no export IDs, no ALL CAPS, no "New Folder".
- Keep series in order (part 1, 2, 3), not in file order.
- Histories go oldest first. Most other lists go newest first.
- Don't show empty things.

## Pages

- Keep pages calm and minimal: few colors, no slogans, no decorative icons.
- Keep long or personal text folded until it's opened, and make sure anything that opens can close again.
- For scans and layouts, show the picture itself along with the text read from it.
- Make tables look like tables. Layout made with spaces becomes a separator. Raw code, markup and broken characters should never reach a page.
- Defaults should work without touching anything. A filter offers a list, not a blank box.

## Truth

- Check every claim against the original before keeping it. If the original doesn't support it, remove it.
- Check medical, legal and money documents line by line. Don't trust machine transcripts of them.
- Other people's words stay theirs.

## Reviewing a page

1. Look at it the way you would on a bad day. Read every line.
2. Open several items and compare them with the originals.
3. Try every button and filter.
4. Fix each mistake everywhere it appears, in the corrections layer, so the fix survives a rebuild.
5. Look again after the fix.
6. Write down any decision you made ("receipts go under money") so the next review doesn't undo it.
