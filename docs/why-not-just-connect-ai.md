# Why not just give an AI access to my accounts?

You can. Assistants can already read your Gmail, Photos and search history, and more connectors appear every month. That's convenient for quick questions about last month. It doesn't replace an archive, for the reasons below, and the two work well together.

## Accounts can disappear

- Services shut down. Google+ closed. Rewind and Limitless, two "AI memory" apps, were shut down in December 2025 after Meta bought Limitless. Data that lived only there is gone.
- Accounts get lost: a forgotten password, a phone number you no longer have for two-factor codes, a banned account, a country that blocks the service, a friend who deletes your shared chat.
- Someone with memory loss, or a family after a death, often can't get into the accounts at all. Terms of service usually forbid sharing passwords, and the accounts of people who have died are locked or memorialized.

An export on your own drive, backed up, doesn't need a password, a subscription, or the company to still exist.

## Connectors see only part of your life

- Many sources have no connector: WhatsApp, paper documents, old phones and drives, bank PDFs, photos from before the cloud.
- The connectors that exist see recent items, some fields and keyword search. Twenty years don't fit in one AI conversation. A connector won't read the text inside your scans, transcribe your voice messages, or read where a photo was taken.

An archive reads everything once (scans, voice, photos), organizes it, and keeps the result.

## Mistakes can't be corrected

An assistant reading raw accounts starts from scratch on every question, so it repeats the same mistakes:

- It counts a friend's forwarded post as your opinion.
- It believes your CV over what really happened.
- It confuses two people with the same name.
- It misreads a name in a scan.

You can't fix these in your Gmail. In an archive you correct each one once, and the correction is applied again on every rebuild.

## Answers are harder to check

An archive gives the same answer to the same question, with the sources under it. A live search over accounts can give a different answer each time, and it's hard to see where each claim came from. For health, money, legal or immigration questions you need the source document, not a summary.

## Privacy

Connecting an AI to all your accounts sends your data through someone else's servers again and again, along with your friends' messages, which they never agreed to share. With an archive you decide what leaves your computer and for which job, and you can use a local model for the rest.

## Lock-in

An archive in open formats (JSON, CSV, SQLite, PDF) works with any AI: one of today's, a local one, or a future one. You can point the assistant you like at your archive instead of handing it your accounts.

## Handing it on

You can give an archive to your children, a carer or a lawyer. You can't legally hand over your accounts.

## Using both

Use connectors for quick questions about recent things, and build the archive as the complete, corrected copy that you own. This repository covers how to make the archive. Any AI can then work from it.
